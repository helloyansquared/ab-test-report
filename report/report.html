Does the one-page checkout sell more?

1. Is the test itself trustworthy?

  • Traffic split: 20,925 vs 20,875 visitors. A sample-ratio check against 50/50 gives p = 0.81, so no sign of a broken randomiser.
  • Sensitivity: with this sample, the test could reliably detect (80% power) a lift of about +15% or more. The observed lift is right at that size, so the result is real but the exact number is uncertain.

2. Conversion

B converts 3.89% of visitors vs 3.38% for A: +0.51 percentage points (95% CI +0.15 to +0.87). Two-proportion z-test p = 0.006.

3. Money, not just clicks

  • Revenue per visitor: €2.48 → €2.79. Bootstrap 95% CI for the difference €+0.01 to €+0.61; Welch t-test p = 0.054. Likely positive, but not as solid as the conversion result.
  • Average order value: €73.53 → €71.71 (CI €-6.28 to €+2.62). Buyers in B may spend a little less per order, a plausible side effect of showing fewer add-ons. Worth monitoring after launch.

The gain is clearest on mobile (+23%, Holm-adjusted p = 0.011). On desktop the lift is +8% and not significant. A formal interaction test (logistic regression) gives p = 0.18, so we cannot claim the effect truly differs by device. Segment results are corrected for multiple comparisons (Holm).

5. Watch out for early results

On day 2 the cumulative lift read +46% with p = 0.010. Stopping there would have overstated the gain about threefold. Days 1–3 show +23%; days 4–14 show +13%. That pattern fits a novelty effect, so the steady-state estimate is the safer number for forecasting.

Methods

Sample-ratio chi-square test; two-proportion z-test with Newcombe CI; bootstrap (4,000 resamples) and Welch t-test for revenue per visitor; bootstrap CI and Mann-Whitney U for order value; per-segment z-tests with Holm correction; logistic regression converted ~ variant * device + visitor_type for the interaction; power via Cohen's h at α = 0.05. Python: pandas, SciPy, statsmodels, matplotlib.
